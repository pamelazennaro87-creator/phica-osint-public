# Digital Volunteers, Ethical OSINT & #OSINT4Good

**Purpose:** Map public-interest communities and structured volunteer initiatives that use open-source research, cybersecurity or digital-safety skills to reduce harm.

**Last reviewed:** 20 November 2026  
**Scope:** Publicly documented organisations and programmes. Inclusion does not imply endorsement, partnership, vetting, or guaranteed volunteer acceptance.

> **Name clarification:** The Guardian Group programme located in the sources is **Project 1591® / PURSUIT®**, not “Project 1952”. If “Project 1952” refers to a different initiative, it should be added only after its official identity and source are confirmed.

---

## 1. Why this section belongs in the Phica research project

Image-based sexual abuse, online exploitation and technology-facilitated abuse require more than awareness. Responsible digital volunteers can contribute to public-interest work by:

- organising and verifying information already available from lawful public sources;
- helping locate missing people through approved, supervised workflows;
- supporting anti-trafficking organisations with structured OSINT leads;
- helping survivors improve digital safety and understand exposure risks;
- documenting patterns and preserving source provenance for referral to competent organisations.

This repository is a research and signposting resource. It does not recruit volunteers, investigate individual victims, conduct operations, or represent any of the organisations listed below.

## 2. Organisations and initiatives

### 2.1 Trace Labs — missing-persons OSINT

**Focus:** Crowdsourced OSINT to support missing-person cases and family reunification.

Trace Labs describes itself as a nonprofit that trains people in OSINT and coordinates Search Party events. Its Get Involved page describes ongoing operations and a community workflow for collecting findings. Its rules of engagement are particularly relevant: participants are not law enforcement, should not pursue suspected offenders, and should submit useful open-source information through the organisation's process for review and referral.

- Official website: https://www.tracelabs.org/
- Get involved: https://www.tracelabs.org/get-involved/
- Documentation: https://docs.tracelabs.org/
- Public framing: **#OSINT4Good**

**Relevance:** A strong example of structured, purpose-limited volunteer OSINT. Missing-person investigations are not the same as image-based abuse investigations; the methodology may be relevant, but cases and safeguarding rules must not be conflated.

### 2.2 Guardian Group — Project 1591® / PURSUIT®

**Focus:** Preventing and disrupting sex trafficking, with volunteer OSINT support intended to generate actionable information for law enforcement.

Guardian Group's volunteer page invites people with OSINT skills to support its PURSUIT® team through Project 1591®. A related description from UpStream explains that analysts contribute open-source research and that findings are compiled and passed through Guardian Group's law-enforcement network.

- Guardian Group volunteer information: https://guardiangroup.org/volunteer-opportunities/
- Guardian Group: https://guardiangroup.org/
- Related description of OSINT-for-good work: https://www.upstreamintelinc.com/osint-for-good

**Relevance:** An example of volunteer intelligence supporting anti-trafficking work through an organisation-mediated process.

**Boundary:** Do not independently identify, contact, follow, expose or confront suspected traffickers or people who may be victims. Follow the organisation's vetting, safeguarding, confidentiality and referral procedures.

### 2.3 OSINT Italia — social-impact OSINT

**Focus:** Promoting OSINT, information literacy, online privacy and social-benefit uses of open-source research in Italy.

OSINT Italia describes a community of people from different professional backgrounds and an association oriented toward social-impact OSINT. Its website describes participation in international OSINT competitions, including Trace Labs events.

- Official website: https://osintitalia.it/

**Relevance:** An Italian reference point for community learning and socially oriented OSINT.

### 2.4 Digital Resilience Project — cyber support for survivors

**Focus:** Volunteer cyber and intelligence expertise supporting people affected by technology-facilitated abuse.

The organisation describes volunteer roles covering mobile-device security, home networks, IoT and vehicle security, OSINT, intake and triage, and digital-safety education.

- Volunteer programme: https://www.digitalresilienceproject.org/volunteer/
- Official website: https://www.digitalresilienceproject.org/

**Relevance:** Particularly close to the survivor-safety dimension of this repository: digital exposure, possible monitoring, account/device safety and practical support.

**Geographic note:** The programme presents itself as Australia-focused. Do not imply that its services are available in Italy or worldwide unless the organisation confirms this directly.

### 2.5 Bellingcat and public-interest OSINT communities

**Focus:** Open-source investigations, verification, geolocation and accountability reporting.

Bellingcat is a major public-interest OSINT reference. Its work and volunteer-facing community activity can help researchers learn transparent verification methods. It should be described as an investigative organisation and learning reference, not as a victim-support hotline or a guaranteed open volunteer programme.

- Official website: https://www.bellingcat.com/
- First Steps to Getting Started in Open Source Research: https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2021/11/09/first-steps-to-getting-started-in-open-source-research/

### 2.6 ObSINT — public-interest OSINT guidelines

**Focus:** Ethical and methodological standards for public-facing OSINT investigations.

The ObSINT guidelines were developed by organisations working in public-interest OSINT, including EU DisinfoLab, CheckFirst, Centre for Information Resilience, DFRLab, Open Facto, OSINT Curious and Logically. They emphasise that publicly accessible information is not automatically free of legal or ethical constraints.

- Guidelines: https://obsint.eu/guidelines-for-public-interest-osint-investigations/

**Relevance:** A useful standards reference for this repository's evidence handling, privacy safeguards, proportionality and publication decisions. It is a guidance resource, not a volunteer-response service.

## 3. What #OSINT4Good means here

We use **OSINT4Good** as a broad description of public-interest OSINT, not as the name of one single organisation or a certification.

In this project, responsible OSINT4Good means:

1. A legitimate and clearly stated public-interest purpose.
2. Lawful collection from sources the researcher is authorised to access.
3. Data minimisation: collect only what is necessary.
4. No intrusion, credential abuse, private-group infiltration or unauthorised access.
5. No doxxing, public suspect lists, victim identification or speculative attribution.
6. No direct contact with victims, families, suspects or alleged offenders unless an organisation's approved safeguarding protocol explicitly requires and authorises it.
7. Source preservation, timestamps, confidence levels and a clear distinction between fact, inference and unknowns.
8. Referral to competent authorities or specialist organisations rather than public confrontation.
9. Survivor autonomy, confidentiality and trauma-informed communication.
10. A documented correction and takedown process if publication creates risk or contains an error.

## 4. Volunteer activity: safe and unsafe boundaries

| Suitable, when authorised and within scope | Not acceptable |
|---|---|
| Verify public reports and official statements | Access private accounts, groups or devices without authorisation |
| Record public URLs, dates and source provenance | Download, mirror or redistribute intimate images |
| Identify relevant support and reporting channels | Identify or expose victims, especially minors |
| Analyse public-domain trends and aggregate data | Publish unverified names, faces, addresses or accusations |
| Submit structured leads through an organisation's process | Contact or confront suspected offenders |
| Help with digital-safety education | Encourage vigilantism, harassment or mass-reporting campaigns |
| Support approved missing-person or anti-trafficking workflows | Treat a CTF, online clue or public post as proof of guilt |

## 5. How to assess a volunteer group before joining

Before contributing personal information, time or investigative findings, check:

- Is the organisation's legal identity, leadership and contact information transparent?
- Does it publish a clear mission, safeguarding policy and code of conduct?
- Does it explain who reviews volunteer findings and how referrals are made?
- Does it define prohibited activity and data-handling / retention rules?
- Does it have a process for errors, complaints and removal of harmful material?
- Does it require unnecessary identity documents, payment, access to personal accounts or secrecy?
- Does it promise law-enforcement access, guaranteed outcomes, paid work or certification without verifiable evidence?

Do not send sensitive victim data to an unverified group. A Discord server, Telegram channel or social-media account is not, by itself, proof of a legitimate organisation.
