# International Comparative Cases — Image-Based Sexual Abuse

**Review date:** 20 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Compare documented international cases relevant to the Phica research. This is a typology and comparative evidence file, not a claim that the cases share operators, infrastructure or a common criminal network.

> **Core caution:** Similar conduct, platform use or naming conventions do not establish operational links. No cross-case attribution is made in this document.

## 1. Why compare these cases?

The Phica case sits within a wider range of image-based sexual abuse (IBSA) and technology-facilitated sexual violence. Comparing cases can help identify recurring mechanisms while preserving important differences:

- non-consensual publication of intimate images;
- hacking or phishing to obtain private images;
- coercion, sextortion and trafficking-like exploitation;
- hidden-camera recordings and voyeuristic distribution;
- AI-generated or manipulated sexual imagery;
- monetisation, paid access and audience-building.

These are distinct behaviours and may involve different offences, victims, evidence and legal frameworks. The term “revenge porn” is used in some historical source titles, but this project generally prefers **image-based sexual abuse** because abuse does not require revenge or a former intimate relationship.

## 2. Case comparison

### Case A — IsAnyoneUp (United States, 2010–2012; federal proceedings 2014–2015)

**Documented mechanism:** The operator of the now-defunct website published nude or sexually explicit images without victims' permission. The U.S. Department of Justice reported that the operator admitted paying a hacker to access Google email accounts and obtain additional images, which were then published on the site.

**Why it matters to Phica:** It is a close historical comparator for a website built around non-consensual intimate-image publication and user-submitted material. It also demonstrates that the acquisition pipeline can include hacking, not merely voluntary submissions by third parties.

**Evidence status:** High for the specific conduct admitted in the federal plea and sentencing record. Do not generalise that every image on the site was obtained by hacking.

**Primary sources:**
- U.S. Department of Justice, 25 February 2015 — [Operator pleads guilty in hacking scheme](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/man-who-operated-revenge-porn-website-pleads-guilty-hacking-scheme-yielded-nude-photos).
- U.S. Department of Justice, 2 December 2015 — [Operator sentenced to 30 months](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/operator-revenge-porn-website-sentenced-2-years-federal-prison-email-hacking-scheme).

### Case B — Nth Room / Doctor's Room (South Korea, exposed 2019–2020)

**Documented mechanism:** Encrypted Telegram chatrooms were used to distribute sexual-abuse material. The U.S. State Department's 2020 human-rights report described women and minors being coerced and blackmailed into producing explicit material, with access sold to users. It reported that the operator of the Doctor's Room was sentenced to 40 years' imprisonment in November 2020.

**Why it matters to Phica:** It illustrates a more severe pattern involving coercion, threats, minors and paid access. It is relevant as a comparison of platform-enabled distribution and audience monetisation, but it is **not equivalent** to the evidence currently documented for Phica.

**Evidence status:** High for the reported prosecution and coercion described in the U.S. State Department report. This summary is not a complete account of all proceedings or subsequent appeals.

**Primary source:**
- U.S. Department of State, 2020 Country Reports on Human Rights Practices — [Republic of Korea report (PDF)](https://2021-2025.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/KOREA-REP-2020-HUMAN-RIGHTS-REPORT.pdf).

### Case C — Telegram deepfake sexual-abuse channels (South Korea, 2024)

**Documented mechanism:** South Korean authorities and media reported Telegram chatrooms suspected of creating and distributing sexually explicit deepfake material using manipulated photographs of women. Yonhap reported on 3 September 2024 that the Korea Communications Standards Commission said Telegram had removed 25 items identified by the regulator and apologised for its handling of the issue. The report also described a preliminary police investigation concerning Telegram's possible role.

**Why it matters to Phica:** It expands the comparison beyond leaked or stolen authentic photographs to synthetic sexual imagery. It also raises questions about platform response, reporting channels and the speed at which manipulated images can spread.

**Evidence status:** High for the regulator's reported removal request and Telegram's response. The existence of a preliminary investigation must not be misrepresented as a finding of criminal liability.

**Source:**
- Yonhap News Agency, 3 September 2024 — [Telegram apologizes for handling of deepfake porn content in South Korea](https://m-en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20240903008900320).

### Case D — MaskPark / Telegram image-sharing groups (China, reported July 2025)

**Documented mechanism:** Reuters reported that a Telegram group named “MaskPark tree hole forum” circulated images of women secretly filmed in public or private settings and private images shared without consent. Reuters reported a snapshot of more than 100,000 users for the named group and described reports of other Chinese-language Telegram groups with much larger audiences. The Associated Press later reported that Telegram had shut down the MaskPark channel and that similar channels had appeared.

**Why it matters to Phica:** This is a particularly relevant comparator for group-based distribution, hidden-camera material, private images and the challenge of responding to activity on a platform hosted outside the jurisdiction where victims live.

**Evidence status:** Medium–High for the reported group and conduct, based on reputable reporting. Membership figures are dated media observations, not independently verified platform analytics. The existence of other groups does not establish that they share administrators.

**Sources:**
- Reuters, 29 July 2025 — [Outrage in China after reports exploitative images were shared in Telegram groups](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/outrage-china-after-reports-exploitative-images-chinese-women-shared-telegram-2025-07-29/).
- Associated Press — [Chinese victims of online sexual abuse face uphill battle in finding justice](https://apnews.com/article/8eea69eccdc0f98db3e349fb68234a18).

### Case E — Celebgate / iCloud photo leaks (United States, 2014; prosecutions 2016–2019)

**Documented mechanism:** The U.S. Department of Justice reported that a defendant used phishing emails to obtain credentials for Apple and Google accounts, accessed private accounts and obtained intimate photographs and videos. The DOJ explicitly cautioned that investigators had not found evidence linking that defendant to the actual public leaks or showing that he shared or uploaded the material.

**Why it matters to Phica:** It demonstrates a critical analytical distinction: unauthorised acquisition, possession and public distribution are separate actions. Evidence that an account was compromised does not, by itself, prove who published the material.

**Evidence status:** High for the phishing and unauthorised access described in the prosecution record. The DOJ's stated limitation on attribution must be retained.

**Primary source:**
- U.S. Department of Justice, 2016 — [Lancaster County man sentenced for hacking Apple and Google email accounts](https://www.justice.gov/usao-mdpa/pr/lancaster-county-man-sentenced-18-months-federal-prison-hacking-apple-and-google-e-mail).

**Interpretation:** The matrix compares what the cited sources document, not the full activity of every participant or group. “Not established” does not mean an activity never occurred; it means the cited evidence does not support asserting it.

## 4. Cross-case patterns worth testing — not assuming

1. **Acquisition is not distribution.** A person who steals, receives, stores, forwards or publishes an image may occupy a different role. Evidence for one role does not prove another.
2. **Platform is not attribution.** The use of Telegram, a forum or a website does not establish shared operators between cases.
3. **Audience metrics are snapshots.** Member counts reported by journalists should retain the source, date and observation time. They should not be treated as audited totals.
4. **Monetisation varies.** Paid access is documented in some cases, such as Nth Room; it must not be inferred in other cases without evidence.
5. **Synthetic and authentic images require separate labels.** Deepfake content should not be mixed with stolen or privately shared authentic imagery.
6. **Victim identification compounds harm.** Publishing names, faces, handles, school/workplace details or intimate content is unnecessary for this public-interest comparison.

## 5. What this comparison does — and does not — establish about Phica

This comparison establishes that Phica can be studied within a broader international pattern of image-based sexual abuse. It does **not** establish:

- that Phica's legacy website operators ran or joined any Telegram group;
- that the reported 2026 Telegram group shares personnel or infrastructure with the 2025 websites;
- that any of the international cases are connected to Phica;
- that every member of a reported group committed an offence;
- that the same acquisition, coercion or monetisation mechanisms occurred in every case.

Any future claim of a cross-platform or cross-border connection requires independently corroborated evidence and a clear chain of provenance. Similarity alone is not enough.

## 6. Research priorities for future updates

- Locate court judgments, official investigation summaries and regulator statements where publicly available.
- Record the exact claim supported by each source, publication date and any caveats.
- Seek independent corroboration before assigning High confidence.
- Track legislative and platform-policy responses separately from criminal case outcomes.
- Add new cases only when reputable or primary sources support a specific comparison.
- Do not access closed groups, solicit material, download intimate images or attempt to identify victims or private members.

## 7. Responsible-use statement

This document is intended for public-interest research, prevention and victim-centred awareness. It contains no intimate imagery, private group links, victim identities or instructions for locating abusive material.

**Compare mechanisms. Preserve uncertainty. Never manufacture connections.**
