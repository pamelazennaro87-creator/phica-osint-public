# Victim Support & Reporting — Italy

This page is for people whose intimate images have been shared, or threatened with sharing, without their consent. **You are not responsible for another person's decision to distribute your image.**

## Immediate steps

1. **Preserve evidence safely.** Keep URLs, account names, dates and times, messages, threats and screenshots of the surrounding context. Do not repost or forward intimate images.
2. **Do not negotiate with an extortionist.** Do not pay, send more images, or agree to meet. Avoid confronting suspected operators.
3. **Report to law enforcement.** Contact the [Polizia Postale](https://www.commissariatodips.it/) or go to a Police or Carabinieri office. Explain what happened, when, where the content appeared, and whether there are threats or minors involved.
4. **Use the Garante's urgent procedure where applicable.** Article 144-bis of the Italian Privacy Code allows a person who has founded reason to believe that private sexually explicit material concerning them may be disseminated without consent to report the risk to the [Garante per la protezione dei dati personali](https://garanteprivacy.it/web/guest/temi/revengeporn). The Garante states that it decides within 48 hours, subject to the procedure and any need for further information.
5. **Seek support.** Consider a trusted person or a specialist victim-support service. You do not have to manage this alone.

## If a minor is involved

Do not download, copy, forward, upload or circulate sexual images of a minor. Preserve non-image evidence such as URLs, usernames and messages, and contact law enforcement immediately. A parent or legal guardian can also make a report to the Garante where the statutory conditions apply.

## Important distinction

This repository provides general public information, not legal advice. Reporting to the Garante and reporting a crime to law enforcement are distinct routes; depending on the situation, both may be appropriate.
