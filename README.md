# Phica Case – Public OSINT Report

**Repository**: Public OSINT analysis of the Phica network (2005–2026)  
**Scope**: Open sources only  
**Purpose**: Document timeline, public identification of actors, and migration patterns of a non-consensual image-sharing network.  
**Disclaimer**: This repository contains only information already published by mainstream media and public statements. It is intended for research, awareness, and support to official investigations. No private data, no operational details for accessing active groups, no doxxing.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Phica (phica.eu / phica.net) was an Italian online forum active since approximately 2005. It specialized in the non-consensual collection, sharing, and sexualization of photographs of women — both ordinary citizens and public figures (politicians, influencers, actresses). Content was frequently accompanied by highly sexist, degrading, and sometimes violent commentary.

- **August 2025**: Massive media and political backlash led to the self-announced closure of the site.
- **September 2025**: Seizure by the Italian Postal Police and Rome Prosecutor’s Office.
- **Late October – early November 2026**: Reappearance as a Telegram group named “Phica – La rinascita” (and variants), which rapidly grew to over 11,000 members before being restricted or deleted.

The case illustrates the resilience of image-based sexual abuse networks and their tendency to migrate to messaging platforms after formal closures.

- **Initials**: V.V.
- **Age**: 45 (as of September 2025)
- **Place of birth**: Pompei
- **Company**: Sole administrator of **Lupotto Srl** (advertising and event organization company). Reported turnover approx. €150,000 in previous years.
- **Online nicknames associated by media**: Phicamaster, Bossmiao
- **Background notes from press**: Previous contact with Postal Police in 2019 regarding the same platform.

All details above are taken exclusively from published journalistic investigations (Repubblica, Corriere della Sera, Domani, and others).

## 5. Platform Migration Pattern

1. Long-running dedicated website (2005–2025)
2. Formal closure + seizure (Aug–Sep 2025)
3. Rapid reappearance on Telegram under a continuity name (“La rinascita”) with the same core activity (non-consensual image sharing)
4. Fast growth facilitated by low barrier to entry and Telegram’s privacy features
5. Subsequent media exposure and platform action against the group

This pattern is consistent with other image-based abuse communities observed in open-source reporting.
