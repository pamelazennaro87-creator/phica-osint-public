# Phica Case — Public-Source Research

**Research status:** Open-source desk review  
**Last reviewed:** 20 November 2026  
**Scope:** Public reporting, official institutional material and documented platform-level events  
**Purpose:** Document the reported history of Phica-related image-based sexual abuse, assess claims about the 2026 Telegram group, and provide safe reporting resources.

> **Core finding:** The Telegram group using the name “Phica – La rinascita” was reported in November 2026. Public reporting does **not** establish that its administrators are the same people who operated the seized Phica websites. Name similarity and similar alleged conduct are not proof of common ownership, control or personnel.

This repository does not host, reproduce, link to, or help locate intimate images, invite links, private groups, victims, alleged perpetrators' personal data, or material involving minors. It is not a substitute for a police investigation.

## 1. Executive assessment

The available public record supports three separate findings:

1. **Legacy websites:** Phica.net and Phica.eu were reported as long-running websites where images of women were published without consent and accompanied by degrading or sexualised commentary. In September 2025, the Italian Postal Police executed a seizure order issued by the Rome Prosecutor's Office. ANSA reported that the person searched in connection with the site was not, at that time, formally under investigation.
2. **Telegram reappearance:** Italian media reported a Telegram group named “Phica – La rinascita” from 30 October / early November 2026. Reporting described rapid membership growth and the circulation of intimate or otherwise non-consensually shared images.
3. **Attribution remains unresolved in public sources:** On 5 November 2026, Open published an interview with a senior Postal Police official stressing that reuse of a known community name does not establish that the same administrators are involved. Attribution requires investigative evidence.

## 2. Scope and research questions

This project examines:

- What can be established from public sources about the legacy Phica websites?
- What did credible reporting document about the Telegram group reported in 2026?
- Which similarities are observable at the level of name, reported content and community format?
- Which claims about ownership, administrators, technical infrastructure or continuity remain unproven?
- How can affected people seek help without further exposing themselves?

It does **not** attempt to identify private group members, map victims, access closed groups, retrieve intimate material, or attribute accounts to real-world people.

## 4. Evidence-based analysis

### 4.1 Legacy websites

ANSA reported that Phica.eu was seized under a decree from the Rome Prosecutor's Office and that the Postal Police searched the person described as the presumed manager. ANSA explicitly stated that the person did not, at that time, appear to be under investigation. la Repubblica also reported the seizure of Phica.net and Phica.eu and described the investigation as concerning the non-consensual circulation of images.

**Do not convert a search, seizure, press allegation or investigative hypothesis into a finding of guilt.** The legal status described by a source must be preserved with its publication date.

### 4.2 Telegram group

Multiple outlets reported a Telegram group using the name “Phica – La rinascita” and described content allegedly shared without the subjects' consent. Reported membership figures are snapshots, not verified platform analytics.

The public evidence reviewed supports the existence of a group reported under that name and the reported activity described by journalists. It does not independently establish who created it, who controlled it, or whether its operators had any connection to the legacy website.

### 4.3 Continuity hypothesis — keep open, do not assert

The following are **leads or similarities**, not proof of common control:

- Reuse of the Phica name.
- Reported similarity in the type of content and user behaviour.
- A migration narrative used in media coverage.

To establish operational continuity, competent investigators would need corroborating evidence such as account-control records, platform records obtained lawfully, payment or infrastructure links, authenticated communications, or other independently verified digital evidence. This repository does not attempt to obtain such evidence.

### 4.4 What this report does not claim

- It does not claim that the old and new groups share administrators.
- It does not claim that all members committed offences.
- It does not identify victims or publish intimate material.
- It does not treat media-reported membership counts as exact current totals.
- It does not claim the Telegram group was deleted or restricted without a dated, reliable source.
- It does not make a finding of criminal guilt against any named person.

## 5. Methodology and evidence grading

Each material claim should be recorded with:

- Claim ID and exact wording.
- Source title, publisher, publication date and direct URL.
- Source type: official / direct interview / news report / secondary commentary.
- What the source directly establishes — and what it does not.
- Corroborating or conflicting sources.
- Confidence: High / Medium / Low / Unverified.
- Last checked date.

### Confidence definitions

- **High:** Direct official documentation or multiple independent, reputable sources agree on the specific claim.
- **Medium:** A reputable source supports the claim, but independent corroboration or primary documentation is limited.
- **Low:** Indirect, ambiguous, single-source or weakly sourced claim.
- **Unverified:** No reliable evidence located, or the available material does not support the proposed conclusion.

Confidence applies to the **specific claim**, not to the overall credibility of a publisher.

### Source hierarchy

1. Official institutional records and statements.
2. Direct interviews with clearly identified officials or primary witnesses.
3. Reputable journalism with named reporting and dated observations.
4. Secondary commentary and social posts, used only as leads and never as sole proof.

### Reproducibility

No claim should be described as independently verified unless another researcher can follow the cited source and see the same relevant statement. Where an article is updated, record the version/date consulted.

## 6. Victim-centred safety and reporting

If an intimate image has been shared or threatened without consent:

1. Do not blame yourself. Consent to create or send an image is not consent to redistribute it.
2. Preserve relevant evidence safely: URLs, usernames, timestamps, messages, threats and screenshots of the surrounding context. Avoid downloading, forwarding or reposting intimate material.
3. Do not confront suspected operators, enter groups to investigate, pay extortion demands, or send additional images.
4. Contact the Italian Postal Police or another law-enforcement office to report the facts.
5. Consider an urgent report to the Italian Data Protection Authority (Garante per la protezione dei dati personali) under Article 144-bis of the Italian Privacy Code where the legal conditions apply. The Garante states that it decides on such reports within 48 hours, subject to the applicable procedure.
6. If a minor is involved, do not save, forward or upload the sexual image. Contact law enforcement and a trusted adult immediately.

Official resources and direct reporting links are listed in [Victim Support & Reporting](docs/VICTIM_SUPPORT.md).
